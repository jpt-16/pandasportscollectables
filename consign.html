The process

Four stages, and what each one costs you

Nothing is charged until a piece sells. If we decline at any stage, you are out the time and nothing else — return carriage is ours.

  1. I

    Submit provenance details

    The form below, with photographs and whatever documentation exists — receipts, letters, prior certificates, a photograph of the athlete holding it. Incomplete history is fine at this stage; invented history is not.

    Reply within 2 business days

  2. II

    Initial review

    A desk review of your images and paperwork against what the claim requires. If it is worth putting on the bench we issue a prepaid insured shipping label, or book you a vault appointment if you would rather bring it.

    Carriage and insurance at our cost

  3. III

    In-person inspection

    The full 18-point inspection in Chicago, plus any third-party opinion the piece needs, commissioned on our account. You receive the written notes either way — including, on a decline, the specific evidence that would change the answer.

    5–9 days on the bench

  4. IV

    Listing agreement & payout

    Reserve, commission band and drop date agreed in writing before anything goes up. The lot is photographed, serialised into the registry, and listed. You are paid once the buyer's funds clear and the returns window closes.

    Listed within 5 days · paid 3 days after clearance

Why pieces come back

The eight most common declines

In rough order of frequency, drawn from the 843 pieces we have returned. None of these is a judgement about you.

  • Secretarial or autopen signatures. Signed by staff with the athlete's authority, or by machine. Legal, common, and not what a certificate can claim.
  • A certificate issued by the seller. Or by an issuer with no accreditation and no archive to check the serial against.
  • Custody that begins at “private collection”. With nothing behind it. We need a first owner, or a credible reason there isn't one.
  • Restored garments. Re-dyed, re-stitched, or fitted with a replacement nameplate. Restoration is legitimate work; it just can't be sold as original.
  • Photo-match failure. The piece is real, the game-worn claim is not. Often a retail replica worn by someone, somewhere, at some point.
  • Cut signatures remounted. A genuine autograph lifted from a programme or card and mounted onto unrelated material.
  • Altered or re-labelled slabs. Cases opened and re-sealed, or serials that do not resolve in the grader's own register.
  • Dating that contradicts the claim. Tag generation, weave, or manufacturer marks that place the piece after the season it is sold as.

A decline is not an accusation. Most returned pieces are exactly what their owner believes them to be and simply undocumented — we tell you what evidence would change the answer, and we will look again if you find it.

Consignor questions

Before you send anything

How long does the whole thing take?

Two business days to a first answer, five to nine on the bench once the piece arrives, and listed within five business days of acceptance. From posting it to seeing it live is usually three to four weeks. Sale timing is the part nobody can promise — our median is 41 days on the floor.

What happens if you decline it?

It ships back to you insured at our cost, with the written inspection notes and a plain statement of what evidence would change the answer. You owe nothing. You are welcome to resubmit if you later turn up the missing documentation, and we keep the notes on file so the second review starts where the first stopped.

Who pays to get it to Chicago?

We do, both ways, insured to your stated value. The label is issued after the desk review, so we only ask you to post something we already think is worth the bench time.

Can I set a reserve?

Yes, agreed in writing before listing. We will tell you honestly where your number sits against comparable lots we have placed, and we will decline the consignment rather than list something at a reserve we do not think it will meet.

When exactly am I paid?

Three business days after the buyer's funds clear and the fourteen-day returns window closes — so seventeen days after sale in practice. Payment is by ACH or wire. If the buyer returns the lot, it goes back on the floor and the clock restarts; you are never asked to repay a settled payout.

Do you ever buy outright?

Occasionally, on pieces we are confident in and want in the vault regardless of how long they sit. The offer will be below what consignment would net you, and we will say so when we make it.

What paperwork do I get for tax?

A settlement statement for every sale, showing sale price, commission band and net. US consignors paid more than the federal threshold in a calendar year receive a 1099-K in January. We are not able to give tax advice beyond issuing the documents.
