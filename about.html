Established March 2011 · Fulton Market, Chicago

Fifteen years, one bench

Panda Sports Collectibles started because a certificate of authenticity turned out to be worth exactly what the person who issued it wanted it to be worth. Everything since has been an argument against that.

How it started

The 2009 jersey

In November 2009, Rey Castellanos bought a signed home jersey from a seller with a certificate, a numbered hologram, and four hundred positive reviews. The signature was secretarial — applied by a team employee authorised to sign on the athlete's behalf. Every part of the sale was legal. No part of it was what the listing said.

The piece cost $340, which is not a story. What it cost beyond that was the assumption that a certificate means something on its own. The certificate had been issued by the consignor. The hologram had been bought in sheets of five hundred.

Two years of buying carefully taught us that the common failure in this market is almost never forgery. It's paperwork: a certificate issued by the party who profits from the sale, a photo-match nobody actually ran, a chain of custody that begins at “acquired from a private collection” and has nothing behind it.

Panda opened in a 400-square-foot unit on Fulton Market in March 2011 with one rule, which has not changed and is not going to: the piece comes to Chicago, goes on the bench, and gets inspected before anyone is allowed to buy it.

Who is actually in the vault

Four people, salaried

None of them is paid a commission on an approval. That is deliberate: an authenticator who earns more by saying yes is not an authenticator.

Philosophy

We don't broker what we haven't held

Four commitments. They cost us listings every month, which is the only evidence that they mean anything.

  • The piece comes to Chicago first

    No drop-shipping from a consignor's garage, no listings built from photographs someone else took. If it is on the site, it has been on the bench.

  • The authenticator doesn't profit from the yes

    Inspectors are salaried and have no stake in a sale. Third-party opinions are commissioned on our account, not the consignor's, so nobody is paying for the answer they want.

  • Rejections are documented

    A consignor whose piece we return gets the full inspection notes and the specific evidence that would change our answer. Roughly one in six submissions goes back this way.

  • The certificate outlives the sale

    Registry entries are permanent and transfer to the next owner. If we ever certify something in error, the refund stands with no time limit, for whoever holds it.
