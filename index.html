Why Panda

Three things we intend to get right

We're collectors before we're a shop. That's going to shape what we stock, how we check it, and what we charge for it.

  • Say what a piece actually is

    We won't write our own certificates — paperwork from the person selling you the item is worth very little, and that's half of what's wrong with this market. Every listing will state exactly what documentation a piece carries, including when the answer is “none”.

  • Stock a collection worth browsing

    Signed jerseys, balls, trading cards, game-used gear and photography, across the sports people actually follow. Rookie cards and modest signed pieces alongside the headline items, because a collection has to start somewhere.

  • Price it for fans

    No buyer's premium, no auction fees, no inflated “estimate” to negotiate down from. The price on the listing will be the price you pay, and we'll keep our margin tight because we'd rather sell a piece to someone who'll display it.

How it will work

Three steps, every item

Nothing complicated. It just gets done the same way every time.

  1. 1

    Sourced

    Stock comes through established auction houses and dealers in the memorabilia trade — not from a supplier we've never met and a photograph we didn't take.

  2. 2

    Checked

    Every item arrives with us first. We check the piece against whatever documentation came with it, and against the condition we're about to describe. Nothing is drop-shipped.

  3. 3

    Listed

    Real photographs, a plain statement of what the piece carries, and one price with nothing added at checkout.
