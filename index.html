Why Panda

Three things we get right

We're collectors selling to collectors. That shapes what we stock, how we check it, and what we charge for it.

  • Authenticity, not assumptions

    Every signed piece we sell was authenticated by an independent third party, and that certificate travels with it. We don't issue our own paperwork and we don't ask you to trust us instead of a real authenticator — anything whose certification we can't stand behind doesn't go up.

  • A selection worth browsing

    Jerseys, signed balls, trading cards, game-used gear and photography, across the sports people actually follow. Rookie cards and modest signed pieces sit alongside the headline items, because a collection has to start somewhere.

  • Priced for fans

    No buyer's premium, no auction fees, no inflated “estimate” to negotiate down from. The price on the listing is the price you pay, and we keep our margin tight because we'd rather sell a piece to someone who'll actually display it.

How authentication works

Three steps, every item

Nothing goes up until it has been through all three. It isn't complicated — it's just done the same way every time.

  1. 1

    Sourced

    Stock comes through established auction houses and dealers in the memorabilia trade — pieces that already carry authentication from a recognised third party before we ever bid on them.

  2. 2

    Checked

    Every item arrives here first. We check the piece against its certificate — that the serial matches, that the item is what the paperwork describes, that the condition is what we're about to claim it is.

  3. 3

    Listed

    The listing names the authenticator, shows the real photographs, and gives one price. The certificate ships with the piece — keep them together, because that pairing is most of what it's worth later.
